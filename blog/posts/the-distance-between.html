The Distance Between

Letting go and staying — three letters, one feeling, written at different distances from the same truth

📅 February 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read 🏷️ Attachment, Letting Go, Growth

Listen twice: first to what's being said, second to who said it.

John Sherman Cooper

Some feelings don't arrive as a single letter. They arrive as three — written at different distances from the same truth, in the same handwriting, but with a different kind of steadiness each time.

This is not a story that moves in a straight line. It spirals. The same scenes return, the same person, the same ache — but each time, the one writing has grown a little more capable of holding it.

Read each column as a different moment in the same journey. The same theme, three distances from it.

Letter I — still inside the feeling
Letter II — beginning to understand it
Letter III — made peace with it
✦ ✦ ✦

I. The Beginning of Wondering

There was no single moment. Only an accumulation — explanations, patience beneath sharp words, a brightness they never noticed in themselves. Somewhere in that accumulation, they became something unnamed.

Is that not something close to love?

II. The Weight of Small Things

There is a word they use sometimes. And every time it lands, something built over years comes down. Not cruelty — just human carelessness aimed at the one place that couldn't afford to be hit.

You once said, "I expect talking to you feels calm." Yet too often I feel read through shadows. That is why I whisper, not demand. That is why my words come wrapped in caution.

V. The Return to Self

At some point, the question stopped being about them. It became: where am I in all of this? This is the moment the feeling meets its limit — and chooses not to destroy.

I like you, but sometimes I resent you. When the only strength I have is shattered by you — I just want to cherish myself more than I cherish you.

Yet still — I cannot erase you. And I do not want to.

Just use it as long as it's there. Just use it until I'm going to make a new life.

naming

Thank you — for letting me feel this. For teaching me what something pure feels like.

In another version of the world, where timing is kinder and God writes our paths side by side — perhaps I would choose you.

But here, in this reality, I hold that truth lightly — not as a request, not as a hope, but as a feeling that harms no one.

peace

This is not a goodbye.

I am simply letting go of one thing. Just one.

The hope of being yours in that particular way — I release it. Not with bitterness, not with regret, but with acceptance.

I do not mind losing the chance to be your lover. But I refuse to lose everything else. You are too important.

Too much connection to be just friends. Too complicated to be a relationship.

Of all the feelings you gave me — the best and the worst — this is the truest thing I know.

I know I am not what you want. But maybe — just maybe — I am what you need. As a friend. As a constant. As someone who sees you, truly sees you, and chooses to stay anyway.

Too much connection to be just strangers. Too complicated to be lovers. So we stay here, in this unnamed space.

And I have made peace with it.

Epilogue: What Changed

"Be imperfect. Because if I am there, it becomes ugly."

That is what the first letter believed. By the third, she had learned something different.

My worth is not measured by being chosen. My worth is not diminished by feeling something for someone who could not feel the same. My worth is in the feeling itself: pure, sincere, transformative.

I felt. I grew. I let go of one thing without losing everything.

And that is enough.

• • •

These letters were never meant to be sent. They were meant to be written — to release what needed releasing, to honor what needed honoring, to clarify what needed clarifying.

The first letter closed a door it didn't know how to name. The second learned to name it. The third closed it gently, with gratitude for the dreams it held — and left every other door wide open.

• • •

So what is this feeling?

Perhaps one day she will find the word. Perhaps she never will. Perhaps it does not matter.

What matters is that she felt it fully, honored it honestly, and chose to stay — not as someone waiting to be loved, but as someone who has already made peace with what is and what will never be.

She is not losing him. She is simply loving him differently.

And maybe that is the closest answer she will ever have.
