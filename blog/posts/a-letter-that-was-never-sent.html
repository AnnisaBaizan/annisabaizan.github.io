Unsent Letter

A Letter That Was Never Sent

A story of questions without answers, feelings without names, and a heart that chose to stay

📅 February 2026 ⏱️ 14 min read 🏷️ Love, Loss, Resilience

Listen twice: first to what's being said, second to who said it.

John Sherman Cooper
✦

A story of questions without answers,
feelings without names,
and a heart that chose to stay.

✦ ✦ ✦

I. The Beginning of Wondering

I don't remember the exact moment it started. Perhaps it was never a moment at all — perhaps it was a slow accumulation of small things: the way they explained ideas I couldn't grasp, the patience hidden beneath their sharp words, the strange brightness in them that they never seemed to notice themselves.

They became my teacher without intending to. My mentor without asking. My devil's advocate when I needed someone to challenge me. My harsh guardian when I was too soft on myself. And somewhere along the way, without permission, they became something I cannot name.

On Unintentional Bonds

Neurologically, the brain forms associations through repeated patterns. What psychologists call "classical conditioning" isn't just about dogs and bells — it's about how our minds link people with emotions through consistent experiences. Each patient explanation, each challenging question, each moment of presence creates neural pathways that deepen without our conscious awareness.

We fall into connection the way we fall asleep: gradually, then suddenly, then inescapably.

From the beginning, I knew I wasn't someone special to them. They said their tone was simply their "natural way," and I believed them. But I also saw how gently they could treat those who truly mattered to them. I understood that difference. I accepted it with a quiet smile, as if humor could soften the sting.

Still, my heart learned to hold them gently.

• • •

Lately I've been wondering what this feeling truly is. Is it love, or care, or empathy — or something softer that only the divine understands? Because whatever name I give it, it keeps returning to them.

If wanting someone to be happy is love — then what is this?

If praying for someone's success without them ever hearing — is that love?

If feeling frustrated when they speak harshly about themselves — what do I call this?

We are not loved because we are beautiful. We are beautiful because we are loved.

Fyodor Dostoevsky

I am afraid to hurt them, so I shape my words softer than I feel them. Not because I want to hide, but because I care about their peace more than my own urgency.

Is that not something close to love?

✦ ✦ ✦

II. The Weight of Small Things

I don't think they're cruel. They are simply human, and my pain comes mostly from the fragile places inside me — although not everything is insecurity. Some things truly hurt.

There is a word they use sometimes — and every time it lands, the walls I've spent years building crumble in an instant. They don't know this, but that word touches the one value I fought so hard to build. I was not born with the shield of beauty that protects so many others. I cannot change my face, so I changed my path instead.

I worked harder than I should have, bled longer than anyone knew, climbed with weary hands and breaking bones just to stand where others arrive with ease.

On Compensatory Self-Worth

Alfred Adler's theory of compensation suggests we unconsciously develop strengths in areas opposite to our perceived weaknesses. When society values physical beauty and we feel we lack it, we may overcompensate through intellectual achievement, professional success, or moral virtue.

But here's the paradox: when someone threatens the very foundation we built to compensate, it doesn't just hurt our pride — it threatens our entire sense of self. The tower we built becomes our only protection, and when it shakes, we shake with it.

This is the only strength I've built to hold my worth. And when it's shattered by someone whose opinion matters — it hurts in ways I cannot explain.

• • •

They often teach me without warning — not because I ask, but because that's who they are. And I accept it because I like learning from them. But when frustration appears if I fail to understand, everything in me becomes quiet. It's one of the reasons I hesitate to ask them anything. I don't want to feel small next to them. I don't want to feel like a burden.

They once said, "I expect talking to you feels calm." Yet too often I feel read through shadows, my meaning slipping away before it reaches them. That is why I whisper, not demand. That is why my words come wrapped in caution, my voice restrained by fear.

Why do I still stay, despite all of this?
Why does my heart refuse to leave?

✦ ✦ ✦

III. The Place You Land

Ironically, I am almost always there whenever their heart is broken. I am the one who stays when the world feels too heavy. I am the one who listens when the silence becomes unbearable.

Maybe I am a place they land — but never the place they stay.

That's why the jealousy comes — not because I want to control them, but because I wish our presence to each other mattered in the same measure. They are close enough to make me stay, but at the same time, distant enough to never choose me.

The worst distance between two people is misunderstanding.

Unknown

Sometimes it feels like they cherish the comfort I bring more than the person I am. Sometimes I wonder if they fear losing me — but never fear hurting me. But maybe that is only my fear echoing back to me.

• • •

Their name is still loud in my head. Close enough to keep me waiting, yet far enough to leave me unsure. It becomes heavier by day. Louder by night.

I see them there in silence, and I make scenarios of them here with me. Yes, I used to create a manufactured happiness — but thank you for letting me see you. Thank you for this strange gift of knowing you exist.

Why is this so bold, so thick, so deep?

✦ ✦ ✦

IV. The Quiet Transformation

If the quiet effort to become a better version of myself — is that not love?

Because without them asking, their presence made me want to grow. I learned things I once ignored. I sharpened my mind. I tried to understand their logic, their worldview, their brilliance — so I could speak with them from a place of balance, not confusion.

If becoming better not to be chosen, but to meet them with dignity — is that not love?

On The Michelangelo Phenomenon

Researchers Stephen Drigotas and Caryl Rusbult identified what they call "The Michelangelo Phenomenon" — the process by which close relationships sculpt us toward our ideal selves. Just as Michelangelo saw David within the marble and carved away everything that wasn't, the people we love help us become who we're meant to be.

Sometimes this happens even when the relationship isn't reciprocal. Their presence alone — their standards, their way of thinking, their example — can chisel away at our rough edges, revealing a version of ourselves we didn't know existed.

They taught me patience I didn't know I had. They showed me the gaps in my thinking I never wanted to see. They challenged me in ways that made me uncomfortable, then grateful, then stronger.

I'm sorry if I can't immediately grasp the way they think. They need to explain their thoughts, to articulate themselves. But one thing they must know — I am willing to understand. I am willing to hear their explanation. It doesn't have to be all at once if they're tired of explaining. We can take it slowly. I'm sorry if I'm slow to comprehend or if I misunderstand.

I am still learning them.

• • •

The feeling I feel towards them is so strong, so deep. This feeling — like a luxury thing. So pure, so sincere. They are special to me.

I am not going to lie — somewhere in my heart, there is a wish. But all the theory they told me gives the same conclusion: we will never be 1+1=11. And for that, I know we will walk separately.

I am not ready for that.

And yet — I must let go of what was never mine to hold.

✦ ✦ ✦

V. The Return to Self

So I remind myself: where is my logic? Where is my faith in these hours when I ache for them?

And the answer comes: return to the Source — the One who steadies hearts, who never wounds with neglect.

The soul always knows what to do to heal itself. The challenge is to silence the mind.

Caroline Myss

For whatever this feeling is — it should not destroy. It must never chain. With too many boundaries, it becomes detachment. But without any boundary, it becomes obsession.

On Emotional Regulation & Boundaries

The concept of "wise mind" from Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) teaches us to balance emotion and reason. When we love someone, pure emotion says "stay at any cost." Pure reason says "leave if it hurts." Wise mind says: "I can love them AND protect myself. I can stay AND set boundaries."

The healthiest love isn't boundaryless fusion. It's conscious choice maintained day by day — choosing connection without losing yourself, choosing presence without sacrificing dignity.

So I remain here, half in hope, half in resignation — holding out my hand into the empty air, wondering if they will ever reach, and fearing they never will.

• • •

I like them, but I resent them. I can understand them, but I can never accept what they do to me. I resent the fact that I feel this way. When the only strength I have is shattered by them — I just want to cherish myself more than I cherish them.

Yet still — I cannot erase them.

✦ ✦ ✦

VI. A Poem Without Answers

Whatever this is — I feel it unconditionally.

Even when what I receive from them
is the ache of being unseen,
the silence that cuts too sharp,
the weight of worry,
the smallness of feeling foolish
when that word touches me,
when that is all the worth I seem allowed to hold.

I know it's not their intention to hurt me.
But this is how it feels to me —
because I hoped too much.

Is this their nature,
or would something more... change them?

I sow seeds with trembling hands,
longing that what I planted
would bloom in sweetness.

Yet perhaps the soil is not the same,
perhaps our roots
were never meant to meet.

✦ ✦ ✦

VII. Letting Go

Now, I don't put hope toward them anymore. But I still like them. I still care about them. Whatever this feeling is — it remains. No matter the condition.

I will never lie to what I feel. I will show it — until it's gone. Just use it as long as it's there. Just use it as long as it's not empty yet. Just use it until I'm going to make a new life.

Maybe now, or forever — it's not my choice anymore.

Some people are meant to fall in love with each other, but not meant to be together.

Unknown
• • •

Thank you — for letting me feel this. For teaching me what something pure feels like. What something true feels like. I hope they are always happy.

Maybe they will still be in my heart forever — in a place that will be forgotten, but never erased.

• • •

In another version of the world, where timing is kinder and the universe writes our paths side by side — perhaps I would choose them.

But here, in this reality, I hold that truth lightly — not as a request, not as a hope, but as a feeling that harms no one and belongs first to the divine before it belongs to them.

✦ ✦ ✦

VIII. What Remains

I feel something for them in a way I know we will not walk the same line — like they always said. I don't hope so, but what if it's true?

I surrender to this truth.

I know I am not what they want. But maybe — just maybe — I am what they need.

• • •

They are still my teacher. My mentor. My devil's advocate. My harsh guardian. My best friend. My inspiration. My motivation. My mirror. My strength. My spark. My chaos and my clarity. My turning point. My lesson, and also my patience.

If being with them means feeling this — then I feel it.

Too much connection to be just friends. Too complicated to be a relationship.

Of all the feelings they gave me — the best and the worst — this is the truest thing I know.

• • •

If breaking yet choosing kindness is love —

If feeling quietly while standing with dignity is love —

If letting go of one role but staying in all the others is love —

Then what is this?

✦

I still don't know.
But maybe I don't need to.

✦
✦ ✦ ✦

Epilogue: A Note to Myself

"Be imperfect. Because if I am there, it becomes ugly."

That's what I used to believe. That my presence would only ruin things. That I was never meant to be chosen.

But now I know — my worth is not measured by being chosen. My worth is not diminished by feeling something for someone who could not feel the same. My worth is in the feeling itself: pure, sincere, transformative.

I felt. I grew. I let go without losing myself.

And that is enough.

On Unconditional Positive Self-Regard

Carl Rogers, the founder of person-centered therapy, introduced the concept of "unconditional positive regard" — the idea that we have inherent worth regardless of external validation. We don't become worthy by being chosen. We don't lose worth by being unchosen.

The act of feeling deeply, of growing through that feeling, of honoring it even when it's not returned — that itself is an act of profound human dignity.

• • •

This letter was never meant to be sent. It was meant to be written — to release what needed releasing, to honor what needed honoring, to close what needed closing.

But some doors don't need to close completely. Some people stay — not as lovers, not as wounds — but as permanent marks on who we've become.

They are one of those marks.

And I am grateful.

• • •

So what is this feeling?

Perhaps one day I'll find the word. Perhaps I never will. Perhaps it doesn't matter.

What matters is that I felt it fully, honored it honestly, and chose to stay — not because I needed to, but because they still matter.

And maybe that's the closest answer I'll ever have.

✦ ✦ ✦

— End —
